lowers it from 35.03 to 34.27 ms (+0.1 fps). The draw counts are unchanged within the run's spread.

The bar was null3D beating or tying three.js on both scenes and both backends. Every figure in
this comparison is a frame rate, and a higher rate is better. On WebGL2 the bar holds: null3D drew
Battle at 48.3 fps against three.js's 32.0, and Factory at 60.0 against 59.5.

The WebGPU query cannot judge the bar. It is not a WebGPU-to-WebGPU comparison. On this Safari the
page reports `WebGLRenderer` for both GPU queries, so three.js's 46.9 and 59.4 are WebGL rates. The
direct path is therefore measured as a GPU-time saving on null3D's WebGPU path, and the bar is
unproven on WebGPU. The GPU-time figures are null3D's own. With the direct path they are 28.25 ms
on Battle and 32.54 on Factory. Forced off, they are 29.10 and 35.03.

The device reports Safari 26.5, where three.js's WebGPU renderer is not used by the page. The R34
