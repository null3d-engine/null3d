## What the figures say

No row moved a frame interval: every one held the display's 16.665 ms.

The wet surface's cost is small. It reads about half a millisecond on the Low preset on every path, and less at Medium. The High and Ultra rows disagree with themselves in ways the settings cannot explain: compatibility at High and Ultra runs at Medium's settings, yet reads 0.88 and 0.55 ms where Medium reads 0.29, and WebGL2's Medium and Ultra read negative. The off side of the same scene also moved between 0.58 and 2.29 ms across the presets. Those spreads bound the run: a difference under about half a millisecond sits inside its variation, and a negative one means the cost is below that resolution.

The planar mirror is small on WebGPU and compatibility, inside or near the variation. On WebGL2 it is not: every preset reads 1.6 to 4.1 ms of GPU time and about 0.6 ms of extra CPU time, against 0.1 to 0.2 ms when the pass is paused. The pass is the driver's extra draw of the whole view, and WebGL2 pays for it on the CPU as well as the GPU. A WebGL2 scene that shows the mirror every frame at Medium is therefore the heaviest case the add-on has.

Both sessions ran in quiet windows; the machine's load sat between 2.6 and 6.1, and each reading waited for the load to fall before it ran.
