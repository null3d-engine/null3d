## The whole look at Medium, on an 8.3-megapixel canvas

The canvas was 1920 by 1080 CSS pixels at pixel ratio 2 and render scale 1, a 3840 by 2160 drawing
buffer, a quarter of the pixels the other cells use. The display interval is 16.665 ms.

| Batch | Look (ms) | Range (ms) | GPU off (ms) | GPU on (ms) | Load before, after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | -0.495 | -0.767 to 1.246 | 4.427 | 3.728 | 4.8, 9.0 |
| 2 | 1.206 | -0.314 to 2.188 | 4.405 | 5.608 | 4.8, 6.7 |
| 3 | 0.846 | -1.306 to 1.523 | 3.622 | 4.209 | 4.8, 4.6 |

The look-off frame read 3.622 to 4.427 ms and the look-on frame 3.728 to 5.608 ms, both well inside
the 16.665 ms interval. The queue is not the problem here. The look's difference is: every range
crosses zero, and the medians disagree by 1.7 ms. At a quarter of the pixels the look costs about
0.9 ms, the same size as the frame's own movement between rounds.

The Medium whole look therefore cannot be measured by the whole-frame method at either canvas, and
each blocks it for its own reason:

- At 33 megapixels, the look-on frame reaches 17.96 and 19.59 ms, past the display interval, so the
  timer counts the queued frame with the look.
- At 8.3 megapixels, the frame fits inside the interval, but the look falls to the frame's own
  movement, about 0.9 ms against about 0.8 ms.

A figure needs a timer that reads the pass, which WebGL2 does not offer, or a frame whose unrelated
preset work is removed until the frame's movement falls below the look.
