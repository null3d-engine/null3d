Plans: the `gust-crossing` page, a probe of the gust that a page sends on a path where the readers
work the air out of the steady field. The path is Creek's WebGL2 path, where the field keeps no

and aligns the two reads to find how far the gust travelled between them. The run is one page in
headless Chrome 156 on this Mac's Apple M3 Max, through the probe's own script.

## What the figures say

The gust travelled 6 m in the 1.000 s between the reads, and the field's own gust speed is 6 m/s.
The two agree, so the air that a page sends now travels across the ground on the path where the
readers meet the steady field, and every reader that takes the fourth settings vector sees it.

The probe measures the air, not a picture: it reads the sent gust's own share, with the base wind
and the swirl removed by evaluating the same place with the gust and without it. Clearing the swirl
matters, because its slow drift otherwise moves a mean place by more than the gust does.

The probe's compute form, which the WebGPU path runs, read zeros, so this run states the WebGL2
path alone. The clause named that path.

## Before the change

The same probe on the branch before this change read no motion at all: the steady field had no term
for a gust a page sends, so a press of G moved nothing on Creek's grass, water or leaves on that
path. D-283 records that, and the change that closes it.
