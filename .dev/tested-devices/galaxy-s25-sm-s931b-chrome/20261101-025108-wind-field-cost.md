## What the figures say

The field costs more here than on the iPad: the shipped cycle reads 0.393 to 0.426 ms at the flat
grids against the iPad's 0.184 to 0.191, and one layered step 0.066 against 0.029. The volume's 40
steps cost 5.243 ms.

That volume row is the one that does not fit. The field steps at a fixed 60 steps a second, whatever
the frame rate, so a frame of this 30 Hz display owes two cycles. One cycle costs about 5.6 ms with
the advection and projection on top, so a frame carries about 11.3 ms of field work. That is 2.2 per
cent of D-241's 499.8 ms figure, which is this phone's measured Creek frame at Ultra rather than a
ceiling with headroom. The frame with the wind is about 510.9 ms, over the ceiling by the same 2.2
per cent. The row is nearly twice the wind's own 6.25 ms figure. At 30 field steps a second the
frame owes one cycle, about 505.3 ms, which is inside the wind's figure but still over the ceiling.
The row is therefore open. The owner must choose one of three options. The first is that the class
ceiling includes the wind's share. The second is that Ultra skips the field on a display that owes
two cycles. The third is that the preset steps down there, which D-241 calls a failure.

High's row is the layered shape at 24 steps and 30 field steps a second: 2.359 ms a cycle, and a
30 Hz frame owes one cycle, so about 2.7 ms with the floor against 4.9. Medium's cycle at 16 steps
is about 0.39 ms, so 0.39 a frame against 1.2.

On WebGL2 the field's cost does not rise above the frame query's floor, and the display's interval
holds at its 33.325 ms cap. These rows bound the field's cost there rather than measure it.
