## What the figures say

Every preset with a field drove the governor down and brought it back. At Medium the share fell
from 1 to 0.75 and then 0.5, the interval doubled from 33 to 66 and 132 ms, the pressure count fell
from 16 to 12 and 8, and the spring trees from 8 to 6 and 4. High stepped the same way. Ultra fell
to 0.5 and then 0.25, with the interval at 64 and 128 ms, the pressure count at 20 and 10, and the
spring trees at 32 and 16.

Ultra is the case that this task exists for. The 30 Hz display owes the field two cycles a frame, so
Ultra's row costs 11.08 ms a frame here. The governor's first step took the row to 32 ms of
interval, which is 31.25 field steps a second, or one cycle a frame. That one cycle costs about 5.8
ms, inside the row's 6.25 ms budget. The page reached the second step too. The settings therefore
make the 30-steps-a-second step reachable, and this page takes it.

The step makes the S25's Ultra row fit the wind's own budget. It does not close D-241's Creek
ceiling on this class: a Creek frame here already measures at the ceiling of 499.8 ms, and the
field's added share of about 5.5 ms still goes over it. The owner's three options for that ceiling
stand, and this task neither changes nor settles them.

The settle phase returned the interval to the display's 33.3 ms at every preset. The budget share
returned to 1 at High, to 0.75 at Medium and Ultra, where the governor was still raising a step,
and to 1 at Low. The frames were inside the budget while the field stayed lightened.

The two captured frames differ in 0.008 to 0.011 per cent of their channels at the presets with a
field. The camera does not move, so only the air can turn the cards. The field therefore keeps
moving while the governor is stepped.

D-283 carries the rows and the budget, and its M3-V16 addendum the governor's floor.
