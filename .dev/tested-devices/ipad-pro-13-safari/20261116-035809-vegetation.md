## The baseline repeats

Each preset has two loads, run in separate device sessions. Ultra agrees to a fifth of a per cent,
796.6 and 795.2. High agrees within five per cent, 697.3 and 729.9, and Medium within thirteen, 704.8
and 796.0. So the multiple, 7.8 to 11 times, is a repeated figure and not one load's. Each load's counts sit in its own row above, because the counts differ between loads and must not be read as one scene.

A third High load read 369.8 ms with 31,700 objects and a first frame of 87.2 seconds. Its late start
disqualifies it under the row rule, and it is not counted. A cloud device's heat and clocks move
between sessions, and that is the likeliest cause of a heavier page running nearly twice as fast. It
is the reading a later person should test.

The scene counts differ between loads at the same preset, from 8,654 to 17,000 objects at Medium and
from 15,600 to 18,500 at High. The add-on builds by the frame, and the settle is a wall-clock wait, so
a load that draws fewer frames holds less. This is likely and not proven: the page reports the frames
of its measured windows, and those follow the settled state rather than cause it. A settle counted in
frames rather than seconds is the repeatable form, and it belongs to the plan's next change.

The CPU column in the first load's pages reads about 2.4 ms, and the GPU timer locates the work
without being read as a share. On one High page the timer reads 891.8 in a frame of 697.3, so that
sample counts more than one frame's work, which is a processor with work queued ahead of it.

## Why the older rows differ

The plan's original pages measure five seconds after the first frame. The demos are still building
their plants then. The same held view held 12,200 objects and 2,000,000 triangles five seconds in
([20261116-035451-vegetation](20261116-035451-vegetation.md)). It held 24,800 and 8,050,000 after
ninety seconds ([20261116-035055-vegetation](20261116-035055-vegetation.md)). That pair found this.

The frame is not explained by triangles. Medium draws 4,570,000 of them and High 7,670,000, two thirds
more, in the same load pair, and their frames differ by 7.5 ms. The plants' drawing is therefore not
what a design has to cut first. The pixels the passes shade are.

This run is the honest baseline. The gap is a design question for the owner, not a tuning target. The
day's levers each move milliseconds against a gap of hundreds.
