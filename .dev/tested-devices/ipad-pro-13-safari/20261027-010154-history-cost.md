The `history-cost` plan runs the compute-state page's cost case on each GPU path. The case adds a 256 by 256 history pass that runs 40 steps in a frame and keeps its state between them, then measures three seconds with the pass running and three seconds with it switched off, in turns. The difference is the pass's own cost, including the swap of its two states and its copy of the newest one. The page reports the frame interval, the busiest thread's CPU time, the GPU time where the device times it, and the pass's texel memory, which it works out from the path and the target's format.

The pass costs 0.49 ms of GPU time on WebGPU and 0.46 ms in compatibility mode. Its compute step is small, so most of that is the two states' copy and the frame's extra work. WebGL2 has no GPU timer on this device, and its fallback draws each of the 40 steps into its own target, so its cost shows on the busiest thread instead: 4.27 ms a frame with the pass running against 0.74 with it off.

The page's texel memory is 2 MiB on both WebGPU paths and 1.5 MiB on WebGL2. It counts four copies at 8 bytes a texel for the WebGPU paths and three for WebGL2, which is the engine's rule rather than a measured allocation. On compatibility mode the target reads `rgba8unorm` while the figure still counts 8 bytes a texel, so that cell is the page's rule.
