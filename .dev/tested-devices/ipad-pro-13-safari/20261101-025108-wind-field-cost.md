## What the figures say

The field's own pass is measured on WebGPU and in compatibility mode. The shipped cycle costs
0.184 ms at flat 32x32 and 0.191 at flat 64x64, and the two paths agree within 0.001 ms. Medium's
row is a 64x64 grid at 16 pressure steps and 30 field steps a second, which is half a cycle a frame
on this 60 Hz display: the 16 steps cost 0.105 ms and the advection and projection about 0.07, so
about 0.09 ms a frame, against a budget of 1.2.

High's row is four layers of 128x128 at 24 pressure steps and 30 field steps a second. Its 24 steps
cost 0.578 ms and one cycle about 0.65, so about 0.33 ms a frame, against a budget of 4.9. Ultra's
row is the volume at 40 steps and 60 a second: one cycle costs about 1.42 ms, which is the row's
figure against the 6.25 budget. The canopy drag adds 0.70 ms to the volume's cycle, because one more read
joins the six that a volume step already makes.

The layered shape's step costs 3.7 times the flat 64x64's, and the volume's 5.1 times, while their
cells are 16 and 24 times as many. A step's fixed cost is therefore most of it, as the Mac's line
says.

On WebGL2 the field's cost does not rise above the frame query's floor at either shape. The frame
interval holds at 15.46 to 15.48 ms, and the busiest thread does not move. So these rows bound the
field's cost there rather than measure it.
