# D-377: One shared generator pipeline; a species is data

Status: decided. Built: the carrier and scale work under way; the pipeline is the owner's highest priority.
Task: none.

Summary: Twenty species grow today through five families' own code beside a planned grower, and the differences between
those paths are where tonight's faults hid: a pine pad placed at a tuft's tip, an ash twig at branch scale, a willow fill
of broadleaf twigs, a tuft that loses its base under a cap, a card level that leaves half its crown open. The owner's
ruling is that the basic process is shared code, and a species is data. Every family emits one intermediate model — axes,
sockets with their carriers, pieces — and one pipeline prices it, grows it, closes it, allocates its levels and bakes its
cards. A contract proved on one species then holds for all, and a fault is fixed once.

## Question

Which parts of growing a plant are the same for every species, and which are a species' own data?

## Data

The same process, five times over: plan, grow to a state, keep the wood under the pieces, allocate each level, bake the
far picture. The same faults, five times over: a piece off its carrier, a piece at the wrong scale, a crown that does not
close, a ring of pieces with nothing between them, a base lost under a cap. Each was found per species, each was repaired
per species, and the repair did not travel — the pine's terminal tuft, the ash's twig scale and the willow's strand fill
are one rule in three species, fixed three times.

The family blocks differ in their structure, not their process: a tuft's stool, a rosette's fronds, a column's ribs, a
culm's pairs and a frond-crown's sprays all reach the same needs — axes that carry pieces, sockets at stated shares, a
crown box from the pieces, wood that follows them, a cover that closes, a card level that stands for it at distance.

## Rule

One pipeline with six stages, each shared code:

1. **Emit.** A family adapter writes one intermediate model: axes (order, girth, course, carrier chain), sockets (axis,
   order, the share along it, the piece and its scale) and pieces (from the record's measured figures). A family may
   not write geometry, materials or levels; it writes this model or it is not a family.
2. **Price.** The model's parts are priced from the record's figures alone: a socket's piece, an axis's segments, a
   clump's cards. A price that is not read from the record is a fault, not a default.
3. **Grow.** Maturity and sparseness keep axes and sockets by rank and grow the pieces' sizes; nothing is grown full and
   cut down.
4. **Close.** D-376's three contracts are enforced here, once: a kept piece's carrier is raised into the drawn wood and
   priced, or the piece is dropped; a piece's scale is the record's; a card level's cover comes from the leaf form's
   own covered share.
5. **Allocate.** The maximum's allowance reaches each part with the crown first at the low maximums, the carriers the
   closure added counted, and every level inside its maximum.
6. **Bake and draw.** The card atlas, the far picture and the levels' draws are shared; a family supplies no bake.

A species is the record, a family tag and its reference store. A change to the shared stages is proved by every species'
digest and the gate's cases; a change to one species is a record edit.

## Consequences

The five family blocks become adapters within one pipeline, and their duplicated seating, carrier, cover and cap code
is deleted rather than repaired. The gate's cases run every species through the same stages, so a new species cannot
miss a contract by being built on a shorter path. Tonight's repairs are re-expressed as record figures where they were
per-species allowances. The migration is ordered by the faults it removes: the carrier and the scale contracts first,
then the levels' allocation and cover, then each family's adapter converted under the suite, with every species' digest
compared at each step. The planned model's thinning, maturity and honest cap are not changed; they become the pipeline's
own stages.
