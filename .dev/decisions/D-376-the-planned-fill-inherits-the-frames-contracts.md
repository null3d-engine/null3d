# D-376: The planned fill inherits the frame's three contracts

Status: decided. Built: none yet. Task: the join and the scale contracts first.

Summary: The deleted frame model built a piece only on the axis that carried it, at the size the record measured it,
under a patch that closed the crown. The planned fill grew straight to a state and priced its caps honestly, but it lost
those three guarantees, and one evening's pictures found the loss in five species: pads hanging a metre off their
boughs, twigs drawn at branch scale, a crown built of a broadleaf's twigs, a bare ring of sprays, and a card level that
leaves half its crown open. The planned fill takes the three guarantees back as contracts of construction, so the faults
cannot be built rather than being caught afterwards. The frame model itself is not restored: it could not thin or cap
honestly, and those are the reason it was replaced.

## Question

Which guarantees did the frame model make by construction, and what must the planned fill inherit to keep them?

## Data

The evening's faults, each measured:

- The pine's pads stood a median 0.50 m and up to 1.12 m off their boughs on stalks, while the bough ran on 0.6 m past
  its pad; the close view drew bare wood as sticks and the stalks as twigs. The fill placed the pad at the tuft's tip,
  whose pivot is not on the drawn wood.
- The ash's finest twigs were drawn 0.65 to 2.6 m long with radii to 5.8 cm — branch scale in a twig's place, with
  leaves only at the tips — against still 22's 35 cm twig.
- The willow's fill hung twigs 0.3 to 0.35 m long at density 8 and 12 on whips that carry strands of 1.1 to 1.6 m; the
  crown read 82.6 per cent wood and 17.4 per cent leaf, and 0.569 open against its photograph's 0.0223.
- The hawthorn's near level left a ring of sprays at one node whose bases stand 0.4 to 0.6 m from any drawn wood.
- The olive's first card level left 0.4472 of its crown open inside the leaf form's outline, against the leaf form's
  0.0620, because its cards stood at one picture a clump where the leaf form's cover needs more.

## Rule

The planned fill carries three contracts, each a construction rule with a test that guards it.

1. **Carrier closure.** A kept row's piece is drawn only where the axis its socket stands on is drawn, and an axis is
   drawn where any of its pieces is. A pass after thinning and after the cap's allowance raises every kept row's carrier
   into the drawn wood at its coarsest own segment count, and drops the row where that cannot be paid. The plan's price
   counts the carriers the closure adds. Test: no kept row without a drawn carrier, and no drawn carrier without a row
   or a stated reason, at every sparseness and maximum.
2. **Scale from one source.** A fill order's length, radius and socket spacing are the record's measured piece figures,
   not retyped numbers beside them. The plan reads the piece's own figures, and the frame's measured spec is their
   source where the record keeps one. Test: each species' fill orders equal its record's piece figures, and a change to
   one without the other fails.
3. **Cover from the leaf form.** A card level's cover comes from the leaf form's own covered share, not from a value
   chosen per species: the bake measures the leaf form's cover and the card level's, and the card level takes the
   factor that brings the two within the round's bar. A record field may state the value the derivation produced; a
   hand-set field does not override the derivation without a recorded reason. Test: every clump-card species' card
   levels stand within the bar of the leaf form's covered share at the crossing.

The cap keeps the closure rule too: a maximum may drop a carrier only together with every row on it, which is the
whole-or-not-at-all rule already recorded for the saguaro's arms.

## Consequences

The body rail becomes a guard rather than the only defence, and its free-row count falls to zero by construction on a
closed plant; the row-sprite picture measure stays as the second check, not the first. The plan's price rises by the
carriers' own triangles, which the caps' allowances then account for; the suite's per-species figures move and are
re-pinned with their reasons. The scale contract makes each species' translation a single edit, and the errors of one
evening — the pine's pads, the ash's and the willow's twigs, the willow's strand fill — become one class with one
repair each. The cover contract moves every card level's openness, so the fitted pictures' per-species table is
re-measured at the same head. Nothing about thinning, maturity or the honest cap changes; the frame model is not
restored, and its spec remains the measured source the planned records read.
