# D-348: Band leaf shape and cluster density, and judge a crown's foliage share

Status: decided; the bands are written into the plant review guide, and the foliage-share measure is being built. Task:
the vegetation rebuild's twelve-row standard.

Summary: Rows 4 and 5 now carry bands, and row 6 takes a second figure. A crown may enclose its reference's share of
sky and still be a lace of bare limbs; the owner's comparison of the common alder's still 02 showed the gap passing at
0.108 against the photograph's 0.101 while the picture plainly did not match. The sky gap could not see it, and the two
rows that could carried no band.

## Question

Two rows of the twelve were unjudged for every species because the standard named no limit, and the row that judges how
much foliage fills a crown took only the share of enclosed sky. Should the standard band those rows, and should row 6
take a figure that separates a mass of leaves from a lace of twigs?

## Rule

A row without a band is not judged, and a species can pass a scorecard while a viewer sees the fault. So: a row that
carries an instrument gets a band, and a row whose instrument cannot see the fault the eye sees gets a second figure.

## Data

| Measure | Passing band | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Row 4, leaf outline overlap | 0.85 or better on each face | The common alder reads 0.919, 0.883, 0.945 and 0.855; the ash's underside reads 0.410 and 0.374, so the band separates a drawn leaf from a painted blob |
| Row 5, cluster sky | No more than 0.10 above the reference's, no more than 0.02 below | The alder's cluster reads 0.335 sky against the photographs' 0.174; the ash 0.579 against 0.390 |
| Row 6, crown foliage share | No more than 0.10 more wood than the reference's | The owner's eye on the alder's still 02: the gap passes at 0.108 against 0.101 while the drawing shows limb scaffolding throughout the crown |

How the data was produced: the figures are the review tool's own, from the species' review rounds; the owner's
comparison is the alder's still 02 pair at the landing head.

## Look

The alder's still 02 decides it. Set the photograph beside the drawing at the same size: the photograph's crown is one
closed mass of foliage, the drawing's is a framework with leaves on it, with a hollow right flank and a trunk too heavy
for what the crown carries. No figure in the standard before this decision reported a fault.

## Consequences

- A species can no longer pass a scorecard with a lace crown or a painted leaf, whatever its sky gap reads.
- The foliage-share measure is owed: the review tool must compute it from the parts picture on the render and from
  colour separation on the reference, and report it beside the sky gap. Until it exists, row 6's second figure is
  measured by hand and the reviewer says so.
- Species judged before this decision are re-read on rows 4, 5 and 6 at their next round. A species whose earlier
  verdict passed rows 4 or 5 as unjudged now owes an answer on both.
